title: Offside
---

## The rule in plain words

A player is in an offside position if any part of the head, body or feet that can legally score is nearer to the opponents' goal line than both the ball and the second-last opponent. Being in that position is not an offence by itself.

## When it becomes an offence

The player is penalised only if, at the moment a teammate plays or touches the ball, they then become involved in play, for example by receiving the pass, blocking a defender's view, or challenging for the ball.

## Where it does not apply

There is no offside from a goal kick, a throw-in or a corner kick. A player in their own half is never offside.

## What happens

The other team gets an indirect free kick where the offending player was when the ball was played. Official wording is in the Laws of the Game published by IFAB; this page is our own summary.
