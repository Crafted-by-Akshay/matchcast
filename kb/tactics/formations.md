## How to read a formation

A formation lists the outfield players from defence to attack, so 4-3-3 means four defenders, three midfielders and three forwards, with the goalkeeper not counted. It describes the starting shape, not where players run during play.

## Common shapes

| Formation | Shape | Typical strength | Typical risk |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| 4-4-2 | Two flat banks of four with two strikers | Compact and balanced | Outnumbered in central midfield |
| 4-3-3 | Three midfielders behind a front three | Width and pressing high up | Space behind the full-backs |
| 4-2-3-1 | Two holding midfielders, one playmaker, one striker | Cover in front of the defence | Isolated striker |
| 3-5-2 | Three centre-backs with wing-backs | Control of the middle | Wide areas if wing-backs are caught high |
| 5-3-2 | Five at the back, three midfielders | Hard to break down | Little support for the strikers |

## Shape changes during the match

Teams change shape when attacking and defending. A 4-3-3 often becomes a 4-5-1 without the ball and a 2-3-5 with it as full-backs move up.
